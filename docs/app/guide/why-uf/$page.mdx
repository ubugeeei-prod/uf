## What you need first

Nothing installed. The pages compare uf with Next.js, Vite, Bun,
`create-react-app` and TypeScript, so they read best if you have shipped a React
application with one of them.

## The pages, in order

<SectionContents section="Why uf" />

The order is the claim, then its price. [What uf is](/guide) is the argument for
one toolchain instead of an assembly of tools, and [Why Flow](/guide/why) is the
argument for the language. [Flow and TypeScript](/guide/typescript) is where the
argument turns against uf, and says so: TypeScript wins most of the rows.
[uf compared](/guide/compare) and [uf and Vite+](/guide/vite-plus) put uf beside
the tools you would otherwise choose, with the rows uf loses in the same tables
as the rows it wins. [What uf does not do](/guide/scope) is every refusal with
its reason and every gap with its issue number, and
[Architecture](/guide/architecture) is the audit uf keeps against its own rules.

## If you read one page

Read [What uf does not do](/guide/scope). A limit you find after a month of work
costs that month, and the page exists so you find it first. If you write
TypeScript and have no reason to leave it,
[How to decide](/guide/typescript#how-to-decide) says who should stay where they
are.

## When you are done

If the answer is yes, [Start](/guide/start) installs uf and builds one
application with it. If you have an application to move,
[Migrating to uf](/guide/migrate) says what moves, what does not, and how to
estimate the cost before you commit to it.
